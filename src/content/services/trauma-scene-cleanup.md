## What Trauma Scene Cleanup actually involves

Trauma scene cleanup is governed by bloodborne pathogen standards, not general sanitation guidelines. Our technicians work in full PPE, use EPA-registered disinfectants and enzymatic treatments rated for bloodborne pathogens, and treat every surface, porous or non-porous, as potentially contaminated until proven otherwise. That includes flooring, baseboards, subfloor material, and sometimes wall cavities if fluid has migrated. Most residential scenes are brought back to a safe, odor-neutral condition within a day, though scope and material removal can extend the timeline. We coordinate directly with law enforcement or the medical examiner's office on scene release before any work begins, and we keep vehicles and crew presentation low-profile out of respect for the property and the neighborhood.

## Our process

1. **Scene release and assessment.** We confirm the scene has been released by the appropriate authority and walk the affected area to determine the true extent of contamination, including areas that aren't visibly obvious.
2. **Containment and PPE staging.** Technicians suit up and isolate the work zone to prevent cross-contamination into unaffected rooms or HVAC pathways.
3. **Biohazard removal and disinfection.** Contaminated materials are removed and bagged per regulation, surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants, and enzymatic cleaners address protein-based residue that ordinary cleaners won't break down.
4. **Porous material evaluation.** Flooring, carpet pad, and subfloor are assessed for saturation. Material that can't be fully decontaminated is removed rather than masked.
5. **Odor neutralization and waste manifest.** We treat lingering odor at the source, document everything removed, and dispose of biohazard waste through a licensed transport and disposal pathway.

## What separates a good trauma cleanup response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating this like a deep-clean job: mopping visible surfaces with household disinfectant and calling it done. That approach misses saturation in grout lines, subfloor, and baseboards, which leads to odor and health issues resurfacing weeks later. A thorough response tests beyond the visible stain, documents what was removed with photos and a waste manifest for insurance and legal purposes, and verifies disposal through proper channels rather than standard trash pickup. Insurance adjusters reviewing these claims look for clear documentation tying the scope of work to the loss, and for proof that waste was handled by a qualified provider, not just a cleaning invoice.
