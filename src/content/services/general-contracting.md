**When a water or fire loss gets mitigated, the hard part often isn't the extraction or the smoke cleanup, it's what happens next.** A bathroom leak gets dried out and you're left staring at exposed studs, a missing vanity, and subfloor that needs replacing before anyone can put the room back together. Or maybe nothing happened at all, and that 1990s kitchen or cramped guest bath has simply run its course. Either way, the gap between demolition and a finished room is where projects stall, and it's the piece most restoration-only companies can't close.

## What renovations, remodels and general contracting actually involves

This isn't paint and trim work. It covers everything from framing and drywall after a mitigation crew has cut out wet or charred materials, to full kitchen and bathroom remodels where cabinetry, plumbing rough-in, electrical, flooring, and finish carpentry all have to land in sequence. A post-damage rebuild usually starts where demolition left off, matching existing structure so the repair doesn't look like a patch. A standalone remodel starts from a blank scope: layout, fixtures, finishes. Timelines vary widely. A single-room drywall and paint repair can wrap in a week or two. A kitchen remodel with cabinet and plumbing relocation typically runs six to ten weeks once permits and material lead times are factored in.

## Our process

1. **Scope of work and estimate.** We walk the space, document existing conditions (including any damage history), and put together a written scope that separates insurance-covered repair from homeowner-funded upgrades.
2. **Permitting.** Structural, electrical, and plumbing work in Henderson and the surrounding valley typically requires permits through the local building department. We pull what's required before framing starts, not after.
3. **Framing and rough-in.** Studs, subfloor, electrical, and plumbing get rebuilt to code. For post-damage jobs, this is where we confirm moisture readings are at acceptable levels before anything gets closed up.
4. **Inspections.** Rough-in and, where applicable, final inspections happen before drywall closes the walls back up. Skipping this step is one of the more common shortcuts that causes problems at resale.
5. **Finishing and punch list.** Drywall, paint, flooring, cabinetry, and fixtures go in last. We walk the finished space with the homeowner before calling the job done.

## What separates a good general contracting response from a bad one

The most common mistake on post-damage rebuilds is closing up walls before moisture content is actually verified, not just assumed dry. Drywall that goes back up over studs still holding 15% moisture content sets up a second problem a year later. On remodels, the recurring issue is scope creep that isn't documented: a homeowner asks for "one more thing" mid-project and nobody writes it down, which is how budgets and timelines both slip.

Insurance adjusters reviewing a rebuild scope are generally looking for three things: that repairs match pre-loss condition (not an upgrade billed as a repair), that materials and labor are itemized rather than lumped together, and that any code-required work beyond the original damage is called out separately, since most policies have a specific provision for that. A contractor who documents moisture readings, photographs framing before it's covered, and keeps permit records organized makes that review faster for everyone, including the homeowner waiting on a check.

## What does renovations, remodels and general contracting cost?

Costs vary enormously depending on scope, finish level, and whether work is a repair or a full remodel. These are typical industry ranges, not a quote, since every project gets its own written scope before work begins. Homeowners insurance typically covers the cost to restore a space to its pre-loss condition after a covered event; it does not cover upgrades, layout changes, or renovations done outside of a claim.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Summer monsoon storms across the Las Vegas valley are a reliable driver of roof and window leaks that turn into reconstruction jobs by late summer, so permitting and contractor schedules tend to back up in that window. Henderson's building stock ranges from older homes that may carry lead-based paint, which is where our EPA Lead-Safe Certified practices apply during demolition and surface prep, to newer construction with stucco and tile roofing that behave differently during storm repair. Extreme summer heat also affects exterior work and material cure times, so exterior-facing projects are often scheduled for cooler months when possible.
