## Do You Call 911 or a Cleanup Company First?

Call 911 first any time the situation involves a death, a violent crime, or an ongoing medical emergency. Biohazard cleanup can only start after law enforcement or the medical examiner releases the scene.

Unattended deaths, suicides, and homicides turn a property into a death scene or crime scene until investigators finish their work. No cleanup company, no matter how fast it can mobilize, can legally or safely begin work before that release happens. Depending on the investigation, release can come the same day or take a day or two. The moment the scene is released is the moment to call a biohazard cleanup company. If a family member needs guidance on what comes next, [Who to Call for Trauma Scene Cleanup in Henderson, NV](/blog/who-to-call-trauma-scene-cleanup-henderson-nv/) walks through that process in more depth.

Not every biohazard call starts with 911. If EMS already responded and left, if you've found needles or sharps in a rental unit after a tenant move-out, if there's a sewage backup contaminating a living space, or if a hoarding situation has created a biohazard buildup, you can call Life Savers Restoration directly. These situations still require trained handling under [OSHA's bloodborne pathogens standard](https://www.osha.gov/bloodborne-pathogens), but they don't require a law enforcement release first.

## What Happens When You Call Life Savers Restoration?

The intake call gathers the property address, confirms whether a scene has been released, and dispatches a technician in sealed protective equipment to assess, contain, and disinfect the affected area under the [IICRC S540 standard for trauma and crime scene cleanup](https://iicrc.org/s540/).

On arrival, the technician suits up in full Tyvek, a sealed respirator, and double gloves before entering. The affected area gets isolated and contained first, so nothing spreads to unaffected parts of the property. Contaminated porous materials, carpet padding, drywall, or flooring that can't be safely disinfected get removed and bagged for transport to a licensed regulated medical waste facility. Remaining surfaces are cleaned and disinfected with EPA-registered products rated for bloodborne pathogens. A final walkthrough confirms the space is safe to re-enter before the crew leaves.

Throughout the job, the crew documents the work with photos and a written scope, both for your records and for an insurance adjuster if a claim applies.

## What Should You Have Ready Before You Call?

Having a few details ready when you call shortens the intake process and gets a technician scheduled sooner.

- The property address and any access instructions (gate code, lockbox, property manager contact)
- A brief description of what needs cleaning and roughly how large the affected area is
- Whether police, the medical examiner, or EMS have already cleared the scene
- Homeowners or commercial insurance information, if you plan to file a claim
- A point of contact on site, even if that's a neighbor or property manager rather than the family directly

## Will Insurance Cover the Cleanup?

Most homeowners and commercial policies cover biohazard cleanup when it follows a sudden, covered event, like an accidental death or injury on the property, but coverage depends on the specific policy language and the cause. It's worth confirming with your carrier before assuming either way.
