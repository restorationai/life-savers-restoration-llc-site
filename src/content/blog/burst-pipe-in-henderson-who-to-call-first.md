**TL;DR:** If a pipe bursts in your Henderson home, shut off the main water supply and the electricity to any affected area first. Then call a licensed plumber to stop the leak and a water damage restoration company to begin extraction and drying within hours. Both calls matter: the plumber fixes the pipe, the restoration crew keeps the water from turning into a mold problem. Document everything with photos before cleanup starts, for your insurance claim.

Water is spreading across your kitchen or bathroom floor right now, maybe from behind a wall or up through the slab, and you're not sure who to call first. In Henderson, where most homes sit on concrete slab foundations with copper or CPVC lines running underneath them, a burst pipe can push gallons of water into drywall, cabinetry and flooring in minutes. The next ten minutes matter more than the next ten hours. Here's exactly what to do, in order, and which call stops the damage versus which call starts the recovery.

## What should I do in the first 60 seconds of a burst pipe?

Shut off the water at the main shutoff valve, cut power to any circuit near standing water, and get everyone away from the wet area if there's any chance water has reached outlets or a breaker panel.

The main shutoff is usually near the water meter at the front of the house or where the supply line enters the garage. If you can't find it or can't get it to turn, that's your first question for the plumber on the phone, not something to fight with while water keeps flowing. If water is anywhere near an outlet, lamp cord, or the panel itself, don't touch it. Flip the breaker for that area from a dry spot if you can reach it safely, and leave electrical work to a professional if there's any doubt.

Once the water is off (or the breaker is), move what you can: rugs, electronics, furniture legs out of standing water. Take a few quick photos or a short video before you touch anything else. This takes less than a minute and it's the single easiest thing you can do for the insurance claim you'll likely file later.

## Who do I call first, a plumber or a restoration company?

Call a licensed plumber first to physically stop the leak at its source, then call a water damage restoration company within the same hour, because drying has to start within 24 to 48 hours to keep mold from establishing. These are two different jobs done by two different companies, and both calls usually need to happen the same day.

A plumber's job is the pipe itself: locating the break, cutting it out, re-piping or patching, and pressure-testing the fix. If the break is under the slab, that can mean tracing the leak with equipment before any cutting starts, which takes time. None of that stops water that's already soaked into your drywall, subfloor, or cabinetry from sitting there and breeding mold while the plumbing repair gets scheduled.

## What happens in the first 24 hours after a burst pipe?

In the first 24 hours, the plumber isolates and repairs the source while the restoration crew removes standing water, assesses how contaminated it is, and sets equipment to dry the structure.

A technician will classify the water (clean supply-line water is generally Category 1, but it can degrade fast if it's been sitting or touching carpet pad and drywall for more than a day or two). They'll use a moisture meter to map how far water has traveled behind walls and under flooring, which is usually further than it looks from the surface. Truck-mounted or portable extractors pull out standing water, and air movers plus low-grain-refrigerant dehumidifiers go in to bring the structure's moisture content back down over the following days. ## Will homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe in Henderson?

Most Nevada homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage from a burst pipe, including the cost to remove and dry out damaged materials, but they typically don't cover the plumbing repair itself unless you've added a specific endorsement.

That split matters when you're getting quotes: the plumber's invoice and the restoration company's scope often go to the same claim but get reviewed differently by the adjuster. Keep the photos from minute one, keep both invoices, and ask your restoration company for a written moisture log, most insurers expect one. The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) has a plain-language breakdown of what standard policies typically cover, and the [Nevada Division of Insurance](https://doi.nv.gov/) can answer questions specific to your policy or help if a claim gets disputed.
