## Is the Water Clean, Gray, or Contaminated?

The category of water determines how the job gets handled, and it isn't always the same answer. A toilet tank overflow (the tank itself running over with fresh supply water) is Category 1, clean water. A bowl overflow with urine but no solid waste is Category 2, gray water. A bowl backing up from beyond the trap, or carrying feces, is Category 3, black water, and it needs antimicrobial treatment and PPE, not just a mop and a fan.

The category also matters for insurance and for what materials can be dried versus what has to come out. If what you're dealing with is closer to a drain or sewer backup than a single-fixture overflow, the approach is different enough that it deserves its own read: see [what Category 3 water damage means](/blog/category-3-water-damage/) before you assume a wet-vac and a box fan will cover it.

## Plumber or Water Damage Restoration Company: Who to Call First?

Call a plumber when water is still actively flowing and you can't find or reach the shutoff; call a water damage restoration company once the water source is stopped and you need the standing water, wet materials, and moisture pulled out of the structure. Most Henderson calls need both, in that order.

The plumber's job ends at the fixture: find why it overflowed (a clog, a faulty fill valve, a cracked flange) and fix that cause. A restoration crew's job starts where the plumber's stops: extracting standing water, pulling moisture out of tile grout lines, baseboards, and subfloor before it reaches framing, and drying the structure to the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/). If the breaker's tripped or there's water near an outlet, that's a job for the utility or an electrician before anyone steps into the room. ## How Far Does Toilet Overflow Water Actually Spread?

Toilet water travels sideways under flooring faster than most homeowners expect, and it doesn't stop at the bathroom door. Tile and vinyl plank look dry on the surface within an hour, but water wicks under the grout line, under the vanity toe-kick, and along the baseboard into the hallway or an adjoining bedroom closet in homes across Green Valley and Anthem where bathrooms share a slab with living space.

A moisture meter, not a guess, tells the technician how far it went. If the bathroom sits on a second floor, upstairs toilet overflow is its own problem: water finds the path of least resistance down through the subfloor, and the ceiling below can look fine for a day or two before a brown stain or a sag shows up. ## When Do Flooring and Drywall Need to Come Out?

Materials come out when they've absorbed Category 2 or 3 water, or when they can't be dried in place without warping or growing mold. Clean Category 1 water on sealed tile or vinyl plank often dries in place with air movers and a dehumidifier. Water that soaked particleboard vanity bases, carpet pad, or drywall from a gray or black water overflow usually has to be cut out and replaced, because it will hold contamination and moisture no fan can reach.

The same goes for baseboards and the bottom few inches of drywall if the water sat long enough to wick upward. A technician checks moisture content before deciding, rather than tearing out more than the job needs.

## What Happens When You Call

When you call, expect the dispatcher to ask what kind of overflow it was, how long the water has been sitting, and whether it's reached flooring outside the bathroom. That's used to send the right crew and equipment the first trip, not a second one.

On arrival, the crew extracts standing water, maps the moisture spread with a meter (not a guess), and sets air movers and a dehumidifier sized to the affected rooms. They'll photograph the damage and moisture readings as they go, which becomes your documentation if you file a claim: timestamps, readings, and the extent of the spread, not just a verbal description after the fact. Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage like a tank overflow, but check your policy, since gradual leaks and sewer backups without a water backup endorsement are often excluded (see [what homeowners insurance typically covers](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance)).

Schedule a moisture assessment before you decide the damage is "just the bathroom." A same-day inspection costs you less than discovering a soft subfloor six months from now.
