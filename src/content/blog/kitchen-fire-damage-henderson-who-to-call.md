A kitchen fire is a different animal from most house fires because of what's burning. Grease, cooking oil and food residue leave a sticky, acidic soot called protein residue that clings to cabinets, the range hood and the ceiling, and it smells far worse than ordinary wood-smoke soot. Even a small stovetop fire the fire department puts out in under a minute can leave smoke damage in bedrooms two rooms away, because hot smoke moves through ductwork and open doorways before anyone notices.

## Why Does a Small Kitchen Fire Damage the Whole House?

Hot smoke and fine soot particles travel through HVAC ductwork and open doorways far faster than visible flames ever do, which is why a stovetop fire contained to one burner can still leave a film on closet clothes down the hall. The smoke itself is the problem, not just the flame.

Grease and protein-based smoke (from burned food, not just the cabinet finish) leaves an oily, yellow-brown residue that's harder to clean than dry wood smoke. It settles on cabinet faces, the range hood, countertops and ceiling texture, and it keeps off-gassing odor until it's actually removed, not just wiped. Standard household cleaners often smear it instead of lifting it, which is one reason DIY cleanup on a kitchen fire tends to make the odor worse before it gets better.

## Who Do You Call First After a Kitchen Fire in Henderson?

Call 911 or your local fire department first if there's any active flame, a smoke detector you can't silence, or a gas smell in the house. Do not go back inside until the fire department says it's safe.

Once you smell gas or see damaged wiring near the stove or range hood, that's a call to the utility company and an electrician before anyone touches a switch or appliance. Don't flip breakers back on yourself.

After the fire department clears the home for entry, that's when a restoration company should be your next call, ideally before you start wiping soot off cabinets yourself. Scrubbing protein residue with the wrong product tends to grind it deeper into wood grain and grout lines, and it can make the odor harder to remove later. Life Savers Restoration handles the soot cleanup, the smoke odor, and any water left behind from the extinguisher or the fire department's hose line, as one coordinated job instead of three separate calls.

Call your insurance company's claims line once mitigation is underway, not before. Document the kitchen with photos first (the stove, the cabinets, any standing water) so you have a record before anything gets moved or cleaned. Most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental fire damage, but check your specific policy language, since every carrier's wording differs slightly. ## What Happens When You Call a Restoration Company for a Kitchen Fire?

The first call is mostly information gathering: the crew will ask what burned, whether the fire department used water or just an extinguisher, whether the gas or power is still off, and whether anyone is currently able to stay in the home. Have your insurance information handy if you've already started a claim.

On arrival, the crew assesses three things separately: the burned surfaces themselves, the smoke and soot spread into adjoining rooms, and any water left behind. Water used to knock down a stovetop fire usually starts as clean (Category 1) water, but it picks up ash, soot and food debris fast, which changes how it needs to be treated once it's been sitting for more than a few hours. The crew brings HEPA air scrubbers to pull soot particles out of the air, specialty solvent-based cleaners made for protein residue (plain degreaser won't cut it), and moisture meters to check whether water reached the subfloor or cabinet boxes. Porous materials that absorbed smoke odor, like open food containers, soft goods or unfinished wood, sometimes need removal rather than cleaning; the crew will walk you through what's salvageable before anything gets thrown out.
