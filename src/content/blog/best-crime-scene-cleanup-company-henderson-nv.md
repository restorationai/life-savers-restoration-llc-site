## What certifications should a crime scene cleanup company in Henderson actually have?

A qualified company trains its technicians to OSHA's bloodborne pathogen standard and follows the [ANSI/IICRC S540 Standard for Trauma and Crime Scene Cleanup](https://iicrc.org/s540/), the industry reference for how this work is supposed to be done. Ask directly whether the crew has had bloodborne pathogen training under [29 CFR 1910.1030](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1030), not just general cleaning experience.

Life Savers Restoration LLC is an [IICRC Certified Firm](https://www.iicrc.org/) and attests its crews are OSHA trained. A business license alone, Nevada's for example is checked through the [Nevada State Contractors Board](https://www.nvcontractorsboard.com/), doesn't tell you whether a company follows bloodborne pathogen protocol. Ask the question separately.

## How is biohazard waste disposed of legally?

Blood and other bodily fluids are regulated medical waste, which means they have to be packaged, labeled, and hauled away by a licensed medical waste transporter, not thrown in a residential trash can. Ask for proof of this before you sign anything.

A company doing this correctly should be able to describe, without hesitating, how waste is contained on site, which licensed hauler picks it up, and whether you'll receive a disposal manifest for your records. That manifest matters if an insurer or a probate attorney later asks for proof the work was done to code. If a company can't answer this clearly, that's a reason to keep calling around.

## How does the company handle insurance billing and paperwork?

Many homeowners and renters policies include coverage for trauma or biohazard cleanup, often under its own sub-limit separate from general property damage, and some costs may be eligible through a state crime victims' compensation program. Coverage details vary by policy and by state, so confirm specifics with your own carrier rather than assuming.

A company experienced in this work documents everything before, during, and after, with photos, an itemized invoice, and the disposal manifest, so your claim has what it needs without you having to manage the paperwork during an already hard week. ## What are red flags in a trauma cleanup quote?

A vague, cash-only, or verbal-only quote with no written scope is the clearest warning sign. Walk away from anyone who won't put the job in writing before they start.

Other red flags: a company that can't name the standard it follows (S540), one that won't discuss how it disposes of biohazard waste, or one that pressures you to sign on the spot without reading anything. A legitimate company gives you time, even a few minutes, to read the scope and ask questions. If the crew shows up without visible PPE for a job that clearly involves blood or bodily fluids, that's also a sign to stop and call someone else.

## What should you ask before you sign?

Ask these questions before any crew starts work:

- Are your technicians trained to the OSHA bloodborne pathogen standard?
- Do you follow the IICRC S540 standard for trauma cleanup?
- Who hauls away the biohazard waste, and will I get a disposal manifest?
- Will you work directly with my insurance company, and do you provide an itemized invoice?
- Can someone start today, and will the vehicle be unmarked?
- What's included in the written scope, and what happens if more is found once work begins?

A company confident in its process will answer all six without hesitation.
