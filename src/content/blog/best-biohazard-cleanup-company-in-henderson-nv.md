## What should you actually check before hiring a biohazard cleanup company?

Four things matter more than a star rating: certification to a recognized standard, direct insurance coordination, documented disposal of regulated waste, and confirmed availability when you call. A company that can't answer those four questions clearly is not ready for this job.

Ask specifically whether the crew is trained under [IICRC S540](https://iicrc.org/s540/) or an equivalent bloodborne pathogen program recognized by [OSHA](https://www.osha.gov/bloodborne-pathogens). Ask who handles the regulated medical waste manifest after the job, since biohazard material can't go in a standard dumpster. And ask whether the company bills your insurer directly or expects you to front the cost and file for reimbursement; that difference matters a great deal in the days right after a loss.

## What happens during a biohazard cleanup appointment?

A biohazard cleanup appointment starts with a scene assessment and containment, moves through removal of contaminated materials, then finishes with cleaning, disinfection, and air quality verification before the space is released back to normal use. The technician first isolates the affected area with containment barriers and puts on full personal protective equipment. Non-salvageable porous materials, flooring, drywall sections, or contents, are bagged as regulated waste and removed under manifest. Remaining surfaces are cleaned, disinfected, and in many cases re-tested before the room is cleared. Costs for this process vary by scope and square footage; the company's own [biohazard cleanup cost guide for Henderson](/blog/biohazard-cleanup-cost-henderson-nv/) breaks down typical ranges in more detail.
