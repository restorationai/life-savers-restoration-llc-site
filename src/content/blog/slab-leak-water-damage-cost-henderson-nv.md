**TL;DR:** A slab leak in Henderson, NV typically produces two separate bills: $1,500 to $8,000 for a plumber to locate and repair the leaking pipe under the concrete, plus $1,200 to $7,000 or more for water damage restoration to dry the structure and replace flooring, drywall, and baseboards. A typical single-room slab leak caught within a day or two runs $3,000 to $12,000 total. The longer it runs undetected, the higher both numbers climb.

If your water bill jumped by a hundred dollars or more for no clear reason, or you've noticed a warm patch on your tile floor near the kitchen or a laundry room wall, you may already have a slab leak. In Henderson and across the Las Vegas valley, that usually means a copper supply line buried under the concrete foundation has failed, and water has been seeping into the slab and surrounding framing for days or weeks before you noticed. The cost question has two parts most homeowners don't expect: what it takes to stop the leak, and what it takes to undo the damage it already caused.

## How Much Does a Slab Leak Cost to Fix in Henderson, NV?

Most Henderson homeowners pay between $3,000 and $12,000 total for a slab leak, split roughly evenly between the plumbing repair and the water damage restoration. The final number depends mainly on how the plumber accesses the pipe and how much flooring and drywall has to come out.

| Scenario | Typical cost range |
|---|---|
| Electronic leak detection (locating the leak before repair) | $150 - $600 |
| Plumber's repair, reroute line above the slab | $1,500 - $4,000 |
| Plumber's repair, tunnel or jackhammer through the slab | $4,000 - $8,000+ |
| Water extraction and structural drying (one affected room) | $1,200 - $3,500 |
| Drywall, baseboard, and paint repair | $800 - $2,500 |
| Flooring replacement (tile, carpet, or laminate, per room) | $1,000 - $5,000+ |
| Mold remediation, if drying was delayed | $1,500 - $5,000+ |

These are typical industry ranges, not a quote. Every slab leak is different, and [Life Savers Restoration LLC](/services/water-damage-restoration/) provides a written scope of work before any restoration begins, so there are no surprises on the invoice.

## What Are the Signs of a Slab Leak in a Henderson Home?

A slab leak usually shows up as a warm or damp spot on the floor, a hissing sound near a wall or floor when fixtures are off, or a water bill that spikes without explanation. Any one of these is worth investigating before the leak runs longer.

Other signs include a baseboard that's warped or separating from the wall, a musty smell in a room with no visible water, or the water meter still spinning when every tap in the house is shut off. In homes with carpet, a damp spot that returns after it dries is a strong signal. None of these confirm a slab leak on their own, but together they're reason enough to call for an [electronic leak detection](/services/water-leak-detection/) scan rather than wait.

## Why Are Slab Leaks So Common in Henderson and the Las Vegas Valley?

Slab leaks are common here because most Las Vegas valley homes are built slab-on-grade, with copper supply lines running directly under the concrete foundation, and the Vegas valley's hard water accelerates pinhole corrosion in that copper over time. Once a pinhole forms, water escapes continuously into the soil and slab rather than announcing itself with a sudden flood.

That combination, slab-on-grade construction plus hard water, is specific to this region. Homes in Green Valley, Anthem, and neighborhoods near Lake Las Vegas see the same pattern regardless of age: copper pipe under concrete, mineral-heavy water, and years of slow corrosion before a leak becomes visible.

## What Drives the Cost Up or Down?

The single biggest cost factor is how the plumber reaches the pipe. Rerouting the line through walls or the attic, bypassing the slab entirely, is usually the cheaper option. Tunneling under the slab or breaking out a section of concrete costs more because it involves excavation and a concrete patch.

Beyond the repair method, a few things move the restoration side of the bill:

- **How long the leak ran undetected.** A high water bill that gets investigated quickly means less saturation; a leak that ran for weeks means more structural drying and a higher chance of needing [mold remediation](/services/mold-remediation/).
- **Flooring type.** Tile and laminate often have to be removed to access the slab and dry it properly; hardwood is more expensive to replace than carpet or vinyl.
- **Square footage affected.** Water from a slab leak travels under flooring and can show up several feet from where the pipe actually failed.
- **Cabinetry and baseboards.** Kitchen and laundry room leaks often mean built-in cabinets have to be pulled to dry the wall cavity behind them.

Most slab leaks in Henderson start as Category 1, clean water from a fresh supply line, but water that sits for days or contacts contaminated soil under the slab can degrade, which is one reason the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) calls for prompt extraction and drying rather than letting it sit.

## Why Do You Get Two Separate Bills?

The plumber's invoice and the restoration invoice cover two different jobs: one stops the water, the other fixes what the water already did. A plumbing company repairs or reroutes the leaking pipe. A restoration company extracts standing water, dries the structure, and rebuilds drywall, baseboards, and flooring.

Some homeowners assume one company handles both, then are surprised to see two invoices. In practice, most plumbers don't do structural drying or reconstruction, and most restoration companies don't hold a plumbing license to repair the pipe itself. ## Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Slab Leak Water Damage?

Most homeowners policies cover the sudden water damage a slab leak causes, such as the drying, drywall, and flooring repair, but typically exclude the cost of repairing or replacing the leaking pipe itself, which insurers treat as a maintenance issue. If the insurer determines the leak developed gradually from corrosion rather than failing suddenly, they may dispute the water damage claim too.

That's why fast detection matters for the claim, not just the cost. Document the leak, the water bill spike, and the damage with photos as soon as you notice it, and get the restoration started promptly to show you mitigated the loss. Coverage details vary by policy and insurer, so check your specific terms; see [Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage? A Plain-English Guide](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) for the general rules on sudden versus gradual water losses, and consult the [Insurance Information Institute's guide to what standard homeowners insurance covers](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) for more detail.

## What Happens During Slab Leak Water Damage Restoration, and How Long Does It Take?

Most Henderson slab leaks caught within a day or two dry in 3 to 5 days using air movers and low-grain-refrigerant dehumidifiers, following the drying protocols in the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/). Leaks that ran undetected for weeks, or that soaked hardwood or cabinetry, can take longer.

The process generally runs in this order:

1. **Leak detection.** Electronic listening equipment or thermal imaging pinpoints the pipe's location before anyone cuts into flooring or drywall.
2. **Plumbing repair.** The plumber reroutes the line or accesses the slab, depending on the location and your preference.
3. **Water extraction.** Standing water is removed and affected flooring, drywall, or baseboards that can't be dried in place are pulled out.
4. **Structural drying.** Air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture meters confirm the slab, subfloor, and framing are back to a dry standard, not just dry to the touch.
5. **Rebuild.** Drywall, baseboards, flooring, and paint are restored to match the rest of the room.
