If a stovetop fire in your Henderson kitchen just got knocked down by the fire department, you're probably staring at blackened cabinets, a film of soot on every surface in the house, and an insurance adjuster's phone number you haven't called yet. The physical fire might have been contained to one room, but smoke never is. It travels through the HVAC system, settles into drywall pores, and clings to fabric two rooms away. Before you sign a contract, it helps to know what a fire restoration estimate is actually built from, and why two companies can quote very different numbers for what looks like the same job.

## How Much Does Fire Damage Restoration Cost in Nevada?

Fire damage restoration in Nevada typically runs $3 to $8 per square foot for smoke and soot cleanup, and climbs to $15,000-$75,000 or more once charred framing, subfloor, or drywall need to be rebuilt. A single-room kitchen fire with contained smoke often lands in the $3,000-$10,000 range in the Henderson area. A fire that spreads into the attic or pushes smoke through the ductwork into every room of a Green Valley or Anthem home regularly runs $20,000-$75,000 once reconstruction is included.

| Scenario | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Smoke and odor only, no structural char | $1,500 - $4,500 |
| Single-room fire, contained, minor char | $3,000 - $10,000 |
| Multi-room fire, smoke throughout the home | $15,000 - $40,000 |
| Whole-house fire requiring reconstruction | $40,000 - $150,000+ |
| Contents cleaning and pack-out (average household) | $2,000 - $8,000 |
| Emergency board-up and stabilization | $300 - $1,500 |

Every loss is different. [Life Savers Restoration LLC](/services/fire-damage-restoration/) writes a documented scope of work before any restoration begins, so the range above is a starting point for budgeting, not a quote.

## What Drives the Price Up or Down?

The biggest cost drivers are how far the fire spread, what kind of smoke it produced, and whether firefighting water added a second claim on top of the fire claim. A grease fire that stayed on the stovetop is a different job than one that got into the wall cavity.

A few specifics that move the number:

- **Smoke type.** A fast, hot fire (dry smoke) leaves a powdery residue that wipes away more easily than a slow, smoldering fire (wet smoke), which leaves a sticky, odorous film that soaks into porous materials.
- **HVAC contamination.** If smoke pulled through the return vents, duct cleaning and a full system inspection usually get added to the scope.
- **Firefighting water.** Most fire calls in Henderson also involve a water damage component once the hoses come out, which means extraction and structural drying on top of soot removal. (See our breakdown of [water damage restoration costs in NV](/blog/water-damage-restoration-cost-nv/) for how that side of the bill works.)
- **Contents volume.** A furnished family room with electronics, upholstery, and soft goods costs more to clean and store than a sparsely furnished space.
- **Code upgrades.** Older Henderson homes near Seven Hills or the historic core sometimes need electrical or framing brought up to current code during reconstruction, which adds to the rebuild line item.

## What Does Insurance Typically Cover on a Fire Claim?

Standard homeowners insurance typically covers sudden, accidental fire damage to both the dwelling structure and its contents, minus your deductible, according to the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance). Most policies also include additional living expense coverage if the fire makes the home temporarily unlivable.

This is also why fire estimates are written in Xactimate. It's the line-item estimating software that nearly every insurance carrier and restoration contractor in Nevada uses, so an adjuster can compare your contractor's estimate against their own numbers item by item: square footage of drywall removed, linear feet of baseboard, hours of HEPA air scrubbing, and so on. A restoration company that writes its estimate in the same format your adjuster expects tends to move through claims faster than one that hands over a one-page lump-sum quote.

What insurance usually does NOT cover without a separate claim or rider: arson, fires started by an excluded cause, and damage from a fire the homeowner knew about and didn't report promptly. Get the full scope in writing before work starts; it's your best protection if the claim gets disputed later.

## What Happens During the Restoration Process?

A fire loss moves through a fairly consistent sequence, which is part of why estimates can be compared apples-to-apples:

1. **Emergency board-up and stabilization.** Broken windows get boarded, damaged roof sections get tarped, and the structure gets secured the same day.
2. **Assessment and estimate.** A technician documents every affected area and writes the Xactimate estimate for the insurance carrier.
3. **Water extraction and drying**, if firefighting water left standing water or wet materials behind.
4. **Soot and smoke removal** from structural surfaces, dried to the [IICRC S700 standard](https://iicrc.org/s700/) for fire and smoke restoration.
5. **Contents pack-out, cleaning, and climate-controlled storage** for anything that can be saved rather than replaced. See our guide on [contents cleaning and storage](/services/contents-restoration/) for what that process actually involves.
6. **Odor removal**, usually thermal fogging, ozone, or hydroxyl treatment, to neutralize smoke smell trapped in porous materials (more detail in our [odor removal](/services/odor-removal/) page).
7. **Reconstruction** of any demolished drywall, flooring, cabinetry, or framing.

For a longer walk-through of each phase, see our [fire damage restoration process](/blog/fire-damage-restoration-process/) article.

## What Should Henderson Homeowners Check Before Hiring a Restoration Company?

Verify the contractor's Nevada license directly through the [Nevada State Contractors Board](https://www.nvcontractorsboard.com/) and get the full scope of work in writing before any demolition starts. These are the two things homeowners most often skip when they're standing in a smoke-damaged house trying to make a fast decision, and both protect you if the claim gets contested later.

The [Nevada State Contractors Board's consumer checklist](https://www.nvcontractorsboard.com/resources/consumer-checklist/) is a short, practical reference for confirming a license is active and in good standing before you sign anything. A written scope should list every room, every task, and the materials involved, not just a total dollar figure.

Fire restoration isn't a job to shop purely on price. The cheapest estimate that skips duct testing or under-scopes the smoke cleanup usually costs more in round two, once the smell comes back.
