## Why Do AC Units Leak in Henderson Attics and Closets?

Most AC leaks in Henderson homes trace back to one of three things: a clogged condensate drain line, a rusted or cracked drain pan, or a frozen evaporator coil that thaws and overflows the pan all at once.

The condensate line carries water away from the indoor coil, and in the Las Vegas valley's dust and hard water, algae and mineral scale build up inside that line faster than in milder climates. Once it clogs, the pan fills and overflows onto the attic floor or closet subfloor. A rusted secondary drain pan, common in older air handlers, does the same thing once corrosion opens a seam. Either way, the water doesn't announce itself until it shows up as a brown ring on the ceiling below or a soft spot in the closet floor.

## AC Leak or Water Heater Leak: How to Tell the Difference

Location is the fastest way to tell: AC leaks show up near the air handler, usually in an attic, a hall closet, or a garage closet, while water heater leaks pool at the base of the tank itself, wherever it's installed.

AC condensate water is typically clear and odorless when it first appears, since it starts as clean, sanitary water condensing off a cold coil (what the [IICRC's S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) classifies as Category 1 water). A water heater leak can also start clean if it's a fresh tank failure or fitting leak. What changes the picture is how long the water sits: water trapped in insulation or behind drywall for more than a day or two starts breaking down building materials and can shift toward Category 2 contamination, regardless of which appliance it came from. If you're not sure which system is the source, a technician can usually confirm with a moisture meter and a look at the supply lines in minutes.

## HVAC Tech or Water Damage Company: Who to Call First?

Call whichever company can act fastest to stop water movement, which in practice usually means the water damage company first if the ceiling is already wet or sagging, and the HVAC tech in parallel to shut down or service the unit.

A sagging, water-stained ceiling means water is already inside the drywall and insulation, and every hour it sits raises the odds of warping, staining that won't bleach out, or mold colonization. That's a structural drying problem, not a refrigerant or thermostat problem, so it's squarely in a restoration company's lane. The HVAC side matters too: the unit needs the condensate line cleared, the pan assessed, or the coil serviced so it doesn't leak again the moment it's turned back on. Running both calls at the same time, one person on the phone with us, one with their HVAC provider, is the most efficient path. If you can safely do it without climbing into a wet attic, switching the AC off at the thermostat until both technicians have been out stops the leak from getting worse while you wait.

## Will Insurance Cover an AC Leak in Henderson, NV?

Most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, like a drain line that clogs and overflows unexpectedly, but they typically exclude damage from a leak that went unnoticed for weeks or months and is treated as gradual seepage or lack of maintenance.

The practical difference comes down to timeline and documentation. If you noticed a new stain this week and called promptly, that reads as sudden damage to most adjusters. If the stain has clearly been there a long time with layered discoloration, the insurer may push back and call it a maintenance issue instead. We've written a longer breakdown of how that distinction plays out in our guide, [does homeowners insurance cover water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/), and our crews document moisture readings and photos at every visit specifically to support a claim either way.

## What About Mold Risk After an AC Leak?

Mold can start growing on wet drywall, insulation, or wood framing within 24 to 48 hours, according to the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home), and a Henderson attic running 120-plus degrees with trapped moisture underneath insulation is exactly the kind of environment that speeds that up.

That's the real cost of waiting a few extra days to make the call: a drying job becomes a remediation job.
