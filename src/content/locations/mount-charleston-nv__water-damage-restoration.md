## Why Mount Charleston Properties See Water Damage

Mount Charleston sits well above the desert floor, and that elevation changes the kind of water loss we see. Winters bring real snowpack, and when temperatures swing above freezing during the day and drop hard at night, ice damming on roofs pushes meltwater back under shingles and into attic insulation and ceiling drywall. Supply lines in unheated crawlspaces or exterior-wall cabins freeze and split, and because many properties here are weekend homes or seasonal rentals, a frozen pipe can run for days before anyone notices. Summer brings the opposite problem: monsoon storms roll through the Spring Mountains fast and hard, and runoff on sloped mountain lots finds the path of least resistance, often straight toward a foundation or walkout basement. Add in the number of homes on private wells and septic systems rather than municipal utilities, and water intrusion events here tend to involve more than just a flooring and drywall problem.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Mount Charleston

We start with moisture mapping to find out how far the water traveled, which matters more here than in a flat-lot subdivision because mountain cabins are frequently built on sloped foundations, crawlspaces, or partial basements that trap water in pockets. Extraction comes next, pulling standing water from flooring, subfloor, and crawlspace soil before it has time to wick further into framing. Structural drying follows using air movers and dehumidifiers set up and monitored per the IICRC S500 standard, with readings logged daily until materials return to a dry standard. At this elevation, cold ambient air and thinner atmosphere change how fast dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air, so we adjust equipment placement and run times rather than using the same numbers we would in Henderson.
