## Why Moapa Valley Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Moapa Valley's monsoon season, typically July through September, brings sudden thunderstorm cells that dump heavy rain in short bursts over ground that's been baked hard by months of desert heat. That compacted soil doesn't absorb water the way it would elsewhere; instead, runoff sheets across yards and driveways and backs up against foundations, block walls, and manufactured home skirting. A lot of the housing stock in Logandale and Overton is manufactured or site-built ranch construction on raised foundations, which handles ground moisture differently than a slab home, and skirting or underbelly insulation can trap water against the structure long after the storm passes. High wind gusts ahead of these storm cells also stress roofing that's already brittle from UV exposure and heat cycling, so shingle and underlayment failure often shows up as interior water staining days after the actual wind event.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Moapa Valley

We start with emergency tarping or board-up to stop active intrusion, then move to water extraction, which in this climate behaves a little differently than it does on the valley floor. Low humidity means surface moisture evaporates quickly, but that can mask moisture still trapped in wall cavities, subfloor, or manufactured home underbelly insulation. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to find that hidden saturation rather than relying on how dry a surface feels, then set up drying equipment calibrated for the dry desert air so we're not over-drying materials that were never fully saturated to begin with. Where storms have brought down tree limbs or blown debris into date palm groves or irrigation structures common on valley properties, we coordinate debris removal before full structural assessment.
