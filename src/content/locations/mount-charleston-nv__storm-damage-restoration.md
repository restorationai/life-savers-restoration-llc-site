**Storm damage in Mount Charleston?** We answer 24/7, and our crews are equipped to handle the kind of damage that high-elevation mountain weather causes: downed trees across rooflines, wind-driven snow forcing its way under shake and metal roofing, and water intrusion that shows up days after a storm has already passed through the Spring Mountains.

## Why Mount Charleston Properties See Storm Damage

Mount Charleston sits at an elevation most Las Vegas Valley contractors rarely work at, and that changes how storms behave here. Heavy, wet snow loads stress roof framing in ways that desert-valley homes never experience, and the same storm system that drops an inch of rain in Henderson can dump a foot of snow at this altitude. Steep, forested lots mean wind events bring down mature pines and firs onto roofs, decks, and power lines. Cabins and homes built decades ago for seasonal use often have older roof decking and less insulation than newer construction, so when a storm cracks a shingle or shifts a flashing seam, water finds its way into the attic space fast, and it can sit there unnoticed until a homeowner spots a stain on the ceiling below.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Mount Charleston

We start with a roof-to-foundation assessment, because storm damage at this elevation rarely stays where it first appears. Water driven under roofing during a snowmelt or wind event tracks along rafters and can surface several feet from the actual breach point. Our crews tarp and board exposed openings first to stop ongoing intrusion, then move to extraction if water has reached interior drywall, insulation, or flooring. Structural drying is monitored with moisture meters rather than guesswork, following IICRC S500 drying principles, because mountain cabins with tongue-and-groove paneling or older subfloor materials dry at a different rate than standard drywall construction. Tree and debris removal is coordinated alongside the drying process so the structure isn't left exposed to a second storm while repairs are pending.

## Reaching Mount Charleston from Henderson

Our base is in Henderson, and we schedule storm response runs up the mountain with winter driving conditions factored into the plan. Access roads into Mount Charleston can close or slow significantly during active snowfall, so when a storm is forecast, we coordinate timing with property owners ahead of the weather window rather than after it, and we stage equipment so crews aren't caught without supplies if road conditions shift mid-job.

## Mount Charleston Insurance & HOA Coordination

Many Mount Charleston properties are secondary or seasonal residences, which changes how insurance claims get handled: carriers often want documentation of when damage was discovered, not just when the storm occurred, since owners aren't always on-site daily. We photograph and log damage thoroughly at first contact and work directly with adjusters so a vacant or part-time residence doesn't become a reason for a claim to get questioned. For properties under a homeowners association or community covenant, we keep repairs within the exterior material and color guidelines those associations typically require for mountain-community homes, so owners aren't facing a second round of work to satisfy architectural review.

## Local note

One thing that catches new homeowners off guard: snow that looks fully melted off a Mount Charleston roof can still be sitting as ice in valleys and behind parapet walls, and that trapped moisture is often the real source of a leak that shows up on a sunny day, well after the storm itself has cleared. We check those hidden pockets as part of every storm inspection here, not just the obviously damaged sections.
