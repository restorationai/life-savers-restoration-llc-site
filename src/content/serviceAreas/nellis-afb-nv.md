## Restoration emergencies common in Nellis AFB

Nellis AFB sits in the northeast Las Vegas valley, and the restoration calls here follow the desert's own logic. Summer monsoon cells drop heavy rain in short bursts, and because the ground is baked hard for most of the year, water runs instead of soaking in, backing up against foundations, window wells, and garage slabs faster than gutters and yard drains can move it. Winter brings the opposite problem: homes built for heat lose humidity control, and single-pane or aging dual-pane windows condense enough moisture to feed mold behind drywall and baseboards if a leak goes unnoticed. Add in the valley's hard, mineral-heavy water, which accelerates scale buildup and pinhole leaks in older copper supply lines, and you get a steady mix of slow leaks and sudden pipe failures across both older base-adjacent neighborhoods and newer construction near the installation.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Nellis AFB

Much of the civilian housing around Nellis AFB dates to the 1960s through the 1980s, built slab-on-grade with stucco or block exteriors and asphalt shingle or low-slope built-up roofing, construction suited to desert heat but less forgiving when water intrudes under a slab or behind stucco. Plumbing from that era is a mix of copper and galvanized steel, and galvanized lines in particular are prone to internal corrosion and reduced flow that can mask a slow leak until staining or a drop in water pressure gives it away. Soil across the valley is largely caliche and compacted sand, which drains poorly and holds water against foundations longer than homeowners expect after a storm. On the installation itself, structural repair work typically involves coordination with base facilities management in addition to any civilian permitting for surrounding properties, and older homes in the area may carry asbestos-containing materials or lead-based paint that a restoration scope has to account for before demolition begins.
